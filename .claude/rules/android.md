# Android app

The Play Store app (`android/`, decided on 2026-10-22, spec "Application Android") was a Trusted Web Activity until 2026-11-12, when it became a Capacitor 8 shell (`capacitor.config.ts`) whose own WebView opens `https://worldup.alng.fr`: no browser, no address bar. There is still no API and no second interface: the server is the same, a site deployment updates the app, and the web version is unchanged. The site uses native plugins only where a WebView can't do what a browser does, behind `isNativeApp()` (`src/lib/native-app.ts`; `appBuild()` there reads the app's build, null from a build older than `@capacitor/app`), each plugin imported on use so that a browser never loads it (import its module and take the plugin from it: a promise must never resolve to a Capacitor plugin, a proxy whose `then()` would be called as a native method): Google sign-in (see "Security and personal data") and notifications (see "Social and market"). `android/README.md` has the setup (upload key, GitHub secrets, Play Console, fingerprints, the Google and Firebase clients), what was edited after `npx cap add android`, and how to try a branch on the emulator against the local site.

- `.github/workflows/android.yml`, run by hand, runs `npm ci` and `npx cap sync android`, then builds the APK and the AAB, signed with the upload key, as a run artifact; the AAB goes to the Play Console by hand. `versionCode` is the run number. Its `server_url` input points a build at staging. `google-services.json` (Firebase) comes from a secret and is never committed. A pull request touching the app builds it unsigned.
- The application ID, `fr.alng.worldup`, can never change once published.
- The position (added on 2026-11-23, for the map's "Je suis ici" and "Autour de moi"): `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` and `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` in `AndroidManifest.xml`, which Capacitor's WebChromeClient asks for when the site first asks the WebView. They need a new release, and the Play Console's data safety form must declare the precise location.
- The launcher icon's long press offers "Ouvrir un paquet" (decided on 2026-11-22): a static shortcut (`res/xml/shortcuts.xml`, its icon `ic_shortcut_open`) whose address `app/build.gradle` builds from the server URL (`shortcut_open_url`, the default collection's `/open`), opened by `MainActivity.openSiteLink`. The web manifest has the same shortcut for the site installed from a browser (`public/icons/shortcut-open-*.png`). Both it and the Haptics plugin need a new release of the app.
- Native side (`android/app/src/main/`): `MainActivity.java` keeps the site's own error pages (Capacitor would show its error page on any HTTP error; here only a network failure shows the offline page, `native/www/index.html`), opens the site's links that come from elsewhere, handles the back button, writes the WebView's cookies to disk when the app goes to the background (otherwise every 30 s or so, and a session opened just before closing the app was lost), and creates the notification channel. `AndroidManifest.xml` lists the paths that open in the app (verified App Links): never `/api/auth/callback/`, so that a Google sign-in started in the phone's browser comes back there. The site doesn't declare `viewport-fit=cover`, so Capacitor's `SystemBars` keeps the WebView clear of the status and gesture bars (and `env(safe-area-inset-*)` is 0 there): declaring it would draw the pages under them.
- Nothing should betray the WebView (asked on 2026-11-16). Capacitor turns its zoom off (`zoomEnabled: false`, written out in `capacitor.config.ts`; the site turns it off too, see "UI"), and it follows the phone's font size. In the app, `html[data-app]` (set by `NativeApp`) turns off what a long press does in a browser: selecting text with its Copy and Share bar, dragging an image or a link; fields keep their selection. The rest holds on any touch screen (see "UI"). Natively (`MainActivity.java`): no scrollbar; other sites open in a Custom Tab over the app rather than in the browser; a file the site sends as an attachment (`/account/export`) goes through Android's download manager with the session's cookies, since a WebView doesn't download; the WebView is transparent until the site paints, over the splash's logo (`activity_main.xml`), so a slow site shows the splash longer rather than an empty screen (Android 12's splash takes `splash_icon.xml`: the launcher icon's logo is in its background layer, which the splash leaves out). The strips behind the status and navigation bars take the ground of the page at the top of the screen (the forced theme, the pack opening's dark stage), with icons that stand out on it: `NativeApp` reads `--color-bg` there on each page and theme change and sends it to the app's own `SiteGround` plugin (`src/lib/app-system-bars.ts`, `SiteGroundPlugin.java`), which an older app refuses, its bars keeping the phone's theme.
- The site's side: `NativeApp` (root layout) marks the page as the app's, hides the splash once the page is drawn, gives the system bars the page's ground and opens touched notifications; `src/app/manifest.ts` (`/manifest.webmanifest`) for installing the site from a browser; the icons in `public/icons/` (from the logo, below; the Dockerfile copies `public/` next to `server.js`); and `/.well-known/assetlinks.json` (`src/lib/android-app.ts`), built from `ANDROID_APP_ID` and `ANDROID_CERT_FINGERPRINTS` in the server's `.env`. Until they are set, the site's links open in the browser.
- The logo (supplied on 2026-10-23 as PNGs from 32 to 1024 px, masters in `design/logo/`) gives every icon. As supplied, on its own cream ground: the favicon (`src/app/favicon.ico`), `src/app/icon.png`, `apple-icon.png`, `public/icons/icon-*.png` and the Android launcher icons (`mipmap-*/ic_launcher.png`). Cut out of its ground (`world-up-cutout.png`): the maskable icon, at 80 % on `--color-bg` so that it stays inside the safe zone (also the Android adaptive icon, `ic_maskable.png`, and the Play Store listing's icon); the monochrome and notification icons (`ic_stat_notification.png`), its light parts in white; the Android splash (`splash_logo.png`); the logo of the visitor home page and of the site header (`public/logo/`, the header's cropped to the badge); and the sharing image, `src/app/opengraph-image.jpg`. A new launcher icon needs a new Android release.
- An iOS app would be the same shell (`npx cap add ios`): it needs Sign in with Apple next to Google (App Store rule), and APNs or Firebase for its notifications.
- Selling in the app (lot 4) falls under Google Play's billing rules for digital goods: decide with the lawyer before payments reach the app.
